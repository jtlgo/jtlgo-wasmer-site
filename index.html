Reading the Purchasing Service Fee on a JTLGO China Order

The purchasing service fee on JTLGO is published in one line: 10% of product value, with a minimum fee of USD 10 per order and no minimum product value. Above USD 10,000 of product value, a specialist can discuss a volume discount. That line is clear, but buyers still misread it on real orders. This article works through several order sizes, shows how the minimum changes the effective rate, and lists the decisions that affect what you actually pay.

Worked examples at different order sizes

The examples below apply the published rule to product value only. Shipping, duties, taxes and special handling are quoted separately and are not part of this fee.

Product value Fee calculation Fee Effective rate
USD 30 10% is USD 3, below minimum USD 10 33%
USD 80 10% is USD 8, below minimum USD 10 12.5%
USD 100 10% USD 10 10%
USD 200 10% USD 20 10%
USD 12,000 10%, specialist may discuss discount USD 1,200 before discount 10% or less

The pattern is simple: below USD 100 of product value, the minimum makes the fee proportionally higher. From USD 100 upward the rate settles at 10%, and above USD 10,000 a volume discount may be available.

What the minimum means for small buyers

If you place several small orders a week, each one may hit the USD 10 minimum. Combining small purchases into one order can lower the total fee. The trade-off is timing: waiting to combine orders delays the first item.

Questions to ask yourself:

  • Do these items need to leave China at the same time anyway?
  • Will waiting push any parcel past the free storage window?
  • Is the saving on the fee larger than the cost of waiting?

What the fee buys

The fee covers supplier communication, purchasing, basic QC photos and videos, consolidation planning, packing requirement confirmation and up to seven days of warehouse storage. That is coordination and checking, not physical warehouse work.

Buyers sometimes assume the fee covers everything in the warehouse. Physical consolidation and unpacking, repacking, packing materials, special handling and storage after seven days follow the warehouse charge sheet. Recognising this boundary prevents the feeling of being charged twice.

Decisions that change what you pay overall

  • Order grouping. Fewer, larger orders reduce the share taken by the minimum fee.
  • Inspection depth. Basic photos are included. Detailed inspection photos, quantity counts and SKU verification have their own prices on the charge sheet.
  • Packing choices. Custom carton sizes, palletising and relabelling may add charges.
  • Storage time. Leaving goods longer than seven days adds storage fees.

None of these changes the 10% rule. They change the lines around it.

Common misreadings of the fee

  • Calculating 10% on the total including shipping. The fee is on product value only.
  • Forgetting the minimum on small orders.
  • Expecting the volume discount automatically. Above USD 10,000, you contact a specialist to discuss it.
  • Treating "consolidation planning" as physical consolidation. Planning is included; the physical work is charged under the warehouse sheet.

How the fee appears on a quote

JTLGO says that before payment it separates product value, purchasing service fee, domestic delivery, additional handling and international shipping. Check that your quote shows these as separate lines. If the fee line does not equal 10% of product value, or USD 10 on a small order, ask why.

The full fee and charge schedule lists both the purchasing fee and the current warehouse charges. Before a large order, the team's quote request form is the route to ask about volume pricing.

Planning a monthly budget

If you buy regularly, estimate monthly fees by summing product value and applying 10%, then add USD 10 for any order expected to be under USD 100. Add warehouse and freight separately.

A small-buyer scenario

A buyer places three orders in one week: USD 45, USD 60 and USD 70 of product value. Each pays the USD 10 minimum, so the fees total USD 30 on USD 175 of goods, an effective rate of about 17%. Had the three purchases been placed as one order of USD 175, the fee would have been USD 17.50. The saving is USD 12.50. Whether that is worth it depends on whether the first item could wait a few days and whether all three would ship together anyway. For buyers who restock regularly, planning purchases into one order per week or fortnight is a straightforward way to keep the fee close to 10%.

A larger-buyer scenario

A business places one order of USD 12,000 of product value. The standard fee is USD 1,200. Because this is above USD 10,000, the buyer can contact a specialist about a volume discount. The discount is not automatic, and the published rule is the starting point.

FAQ

Is the fee charged on shipping costs?

No. It is based on product value only.

Why did I pay USD 10 on a USD 40 order?

Because the minimum fee is USD 10 per order, which is higher than 10% of USD 40.

Does the fee include repacking?

No. Repacking and packing materials follow the warehouse charge sheet.

How do I get a volume discount?

For product value above USD 10,000, contact a specialist to discuss it.
